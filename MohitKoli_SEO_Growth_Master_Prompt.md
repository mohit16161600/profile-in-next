# 7. QUERY-TO-PAGE ANALYSIS

This is one of the highest-priority tasks.

For each important page, identify the actual queries Google is already associating with it.

Do NOT decide the target keyword solely from the URL slug.

Example:

A page called:

`/blog/hostinger-business-plan`

might actually be getting impressions for:

- hostinger business plan
- hostinger business price
- hostinger business hosting
- hostinger business hosting india
- hostinger business vs premium
- hostinger business renewal

The actual GSC query data decides what the page should prioritize.

Build a table:

| Page | Query | Impressions | Position | CTR | Intent | Action |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|

Then identify:

### Quick wins
Queries/pages with:
- meaningful impressions
- position roughly 4–15
- low CTR
- strong relevance
- clear search intent

### Ranking opportunities
Queries around positions 8–20 with meaningful impressions.

### CTR opportunities
Queries around positions 1–10 with unusually low CTR.

### Demand problems
Pages with good rankings but almost no impressions.

### Cannibalization
Multiple pages ranking/impressioning for the same query family.

# 41. EXPERIMENT DECISION RULES

After sufficient data:

### WIN
If relevant impressions/clicks improve materially without harmful side effects.

→ Keep.

### PARTIAL WIN
Impressions improve but clicks do not.

→ Investigate title/intent/CTR.

### RANKING WIN / CTR LOSS
Position improves but CTR falls.

→ Test SERP messaging/title.

### TRAFFIC WIN / CONVERSION LOSS
Traffic increases but affiliate clicks decline.

→ Review intent and CTA placement.

### FAILURE
No meaningful improvement after a reasonable measurement period.

→ Diagnose before reverting.

Possible causes:

- wrong keyword
- wrong intent
- weak title
- weak content
- poor authority
- poor SERP fit
- cannibalization
- low demand
- technical problem
