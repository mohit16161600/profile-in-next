## Title

```
Black Friday hosting maths: the cheapest plan has the worst renewal
```

## Body

```
Every Black Friday hosting post sorts by discount percentage. That column is the one number that cannot tell you whether the deal is good.

I checked an Indian host's own storefront on 16 November 2026 — eleven days before Black Friday, and the sale was already live. Here is the shared ladder, sale price beside renewal rate:

    Plan            Sale      Renews at   Increase
    Single          ₹69/mo    ₹289/mo     +319%
    Premium         ₹139/mo   ₹449/mo     +223%
    Unlimited       ₹199/mo   ₹649/mo     +226%
    Cloud Startup   ₹599/mo   ₹1,599/mo   +167%

Read the last column, not the second.

The ₹69 plan is the one on the banner, and it is the worst deal on the page. It triples and then some. Cloud Startup looks five times more expensive and costs the least to keep. The discount percentage and the value are inversely ordered, and nothing on the pricing page tells you that.

## Three things that compound

**The monthly figure is a division.** ₹69/mo is ₹3,312 for 48 months, charged today as one invoice, plus 18% GST. There is no ₹69 monthly charge anywhere. Take the same plan month to month and the discount effectively disappears — you pay near the renewal rate from day one.

**No discount ever reaches a renewal.** Sale prices, coupon codes, referral codes, Black Friday, Cyber Monday — every one of them is first-purchase only. The cheapest total path is therefore always the longest first term, which is also the largest single payment and the biggest bet that the project outlives it.

**The upgrade path is priced against you.** Outgrow Single in year two and you are not moving from ₹69 to ₹139. You are moving from a ₹289 renewal to a ₹449 renewal, because the intro rate is gone the moment you are an existing customer.

## The VPS side has the same shape, different ordering

    KVM 1   ₹599/mo     ₹999/mo     +67%
    KVM 2   ₹779/mo     ₹1,199/mo   +54%
    KVM 4   ₹1,099/mo   ₹2,399/mo   +118%
    KVM 8   ₹2,199/mo   ₹4,399/mo   +100%

KVM 2 is the flattest tier on the entire ladder at +54%. KVM 4 more than doubles, and KVM 4 is the tier that "recommended for growing apps" listicles point at.

## So what do you actually do in a Black Friday window

The sale was live before the date and will be live after it. Waiting for 27 November to save a few rupees a month on a four-year invoice is optimising the wrong variable.

The variable that matters is the term. The sale rate exists only on the longest one, so the real question is not which week you buy — it is whether you want a four-year commitment at all.

If the project has not found users yet, the correct Black Friday move is the unglamorous one: pay monthly at the undiscounted rate and stay free to leave. It costs more per month and far less in total when the thing does not survive, which is most of the time.

That advice costs me money, and it is still the right advice.

I put the renewal column next to the sale column for every plan here, with the date each figure was checked:
https://mohitkoli.in/blog/hostinger-black-friday-2026

Two questions for this crowd:

1. Has anyone found a host that prints the renewal rate on the pricing page itself, rather than three clicks deep in a knowledge base?
2. Does anyone budget the renewal cliff up front, or is it universally absorbed as a surprise in month 49?

Disclosure: my site carries a hosting referral link. The numbers above are the provider's own published rates, including the renewal rate they would rather you compared later.

The ₹69 plan renews at a 319% increase. It is the cheapest number on the page and the most expensive plan to keep. The ₹599 plan renews at +167%, the gentlest on the ladder. Whatever the banner is sorted by, it is not value.

## The three sentences the pricing page does not contain

The advertised monthly rate is one upfront invoice divided by the term, not a monthly charge.

No discount of any kind — sale, coupon, referral, seasonal — ever applies to a renewal.

The intro rate on a higher plan disappears the moment you become an existing customer, so upgrading mid-term costs renewal-to-renewal, not intro-to-intro.

Each of those is checkable in about ninety seconds on the provider's own site. None of them appears in the Black Friday copy, at any host, in any year.

## What I do now

I stopped asking "how much is it" and started asking "what does it cost in month 49". That single change reorders every comparison table I have ever read, and it usually moves me one plan up and one term down from whatever was recommended.

And when a project is new enough that I cannot answer the month-49 question honestly, I pay monthly and skip the sale. A four-year commitment is a financing decision wearing a hosting decision's clothes.

## BF-2 → "is it cheaper to buy hosting on Black Friday"

Target: `https://mohitkoli.in/blog/hostinger-black-friday-2026`

```
Cheaper than the list price, yes. Cheaper than buying the same plan last week or next month — usually not, and that is the part the countdown timers exist to prevent you from checking.

I looked at an Indian storefront on 16 November 2026. The Black Friday-level price was already live, eleven days out. Hosts run near-continuous sale calendars; the headline number moves within a few points across the whole November–December window.

What genuinely changes your total is not the week. It is the term.

The sale rate only attaches to the longest term, normally 48 months on shared hosting and 24 on VPS. So the decision is not "do I catch the right Friday" but "am I paying four years up front". On a ₹139/mo plan that is ₹6,672 today, plus 18% GST, and the renewal after it is ₹449/mo.

A rule that has saved me money: compare the renewal rates, not the sale prices. On the ladder I checked, the cheapest plan renewed at +319% and the dearest at +167%. The cheap one is the expensive one, two years out.

And if the project has not found users yet, monthly billing at the undiscounted rate is the cheaper decision in expectation, because most projects do not reach month 49.

## What NOT to do, however tempting the volume looks

Directory submissions, "500 backlinks" gigs, blog comments, PBNs, link
exchange groups, paid guest posts on sites that exist to sell guest posts.

More links is the goal; more *domains that Google counts* is the actual goal,
and those lists produce neither. A site with this little traffic cannot absorb
a manual action.

The honest arithmetic: dev.to went from 3 linking pages to 5 and is the only
channel confirmed working. One good post a week on a platform that does not
remove you beats fifty listings nobody reads.
